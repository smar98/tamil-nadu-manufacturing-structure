# ASUSE reported problems: exploratory appendix

This appendix examines associations between the problems that establishments report and their size, age, registration, internet use, loans and GVA per worker. The associations are hypothesis-generating cross-sectional correlations. They are not estimates of constraints on firms. The appendix is separate from the main payload and does not change the site's figures.

## Data and definitions

- **Universe:** Tamil Nadu ASUSE 2023-24 manufacturing establishments (NIC divisions 10-33). Weight = `mlt / 100`.
- **Generated by:** `research/scripts/build_asuse_problems_appendix.py`, which writes `research/derived/asuse_problems_appendix.json`. The script reads only the ASUSE 2023-24 CSV archive and never exports respondent-level records or design identifiers.
- **Reporting a problem:** an establishment is counted as reporting problem category *c* if *c* is its most severe problem or its next most severe problem. An establishment can report up to two problems, in order of severity. Non-reporters are all other establishments in the universe, including establishments that report no problem at all.
- **Registration:** item 227. **ICT/internet use:** item 223. **Outstanding loan:** the establishment's own total outstanding loan balance (Level 12, Block 11, item 1119). Loans advanced by financial establishments (Level 13, item 1121) are a different concept and are not used.
- **GVA per worker:** the ASUSE definition in METHODOLOGY.md, restricted to market establishments with valid GVA.
- **Establishment age:** the banded field (item 214) cannot support a weighted mean. The age used is 2024 minus the year of initial operation under the current owner (item 215). A single reference year is used for every establishment. This is an approximation, because the true reference date varies by sampled unit within the 2023-24 round.
- **Disclosure:** a cell is suppressed when its unweighted sample count is below 10, or when one observation contributes more than 70% of the weighted denominator or of the absolute weighted numerator. Suppressed values are reported as null. Every published cell carries its unweighted sample count. A reporter and non-reporter pair is nulled together if either side would be suppressed, because the residual could otherwise reconstruct the suppressed side.

## Interpretation

These are cross-sectional associations. They do not estimate constraints on firms. Reverse causation and selection are plausible for every pattern above. More productive or more formal establishments may be the ones with enough scale to seek finance, hire staff or notice a skills gap. An establishment that reports a problem is therefore often the more capable one. The report does not show that it is the more constrained one.

The strongest permitted wording is: **"establishments reporting X differ from establishments not reporting X on Y."** The results do not show that X constrains growth, or that establishments are held back by X.

In these data, establishments reporting non-availability of skilled labour, non-availability of labour, or lack of technological upgrade are far more likely to be registered, to use ICT and to hold an outstanding loan than establishments that do not report those problems. They also show higher GVA per worker. This is consistent with these being the more formalized and more productive establishments, for which growth has made labour and technology gaps newly visible (a selection or reverse-causation account). It is not evidence that labour or technology access holds back weaker establishments.

The "others" category shows a flatter or mildly reversed pattern, with slightly lower GVA per worker and ICT use among reporters. "Lack of connectivity to market" is the only category in which reporters have lower ICT use than non-reporters. This cross-section gives no causal reading for that either.

No number in this appendix should be cited as evidence that any single factor constrains Tamil Nadu manufacturing growth.
