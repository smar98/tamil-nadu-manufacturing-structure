# Policy evidence

This note links the project's findings to current and historical Tamil Nadu policy instruments. For each instrument it states what the government reports and what the data cannot show. It makes no causal claim and does not assert any official demand for this analysis.

## Evidence levels

1. **Stated objective:** what an official document explicitly says.
2. **Instrument:** a subsidy, loan, park, facility or programme.
3. **Output:** money disbursed, recipients, facilities built.
4. **Outcome:** employment, earnings, formalisation, GVA, coverage levels.
5. **Impact:** what happened because of the policy, relative to a counterfactual.

The policy documents establish levels 1 to 3. The survey estimates describe some level-4 outcomes. This project does not establish level 5, and no wording here implies that it does.

Survey evidence throughout is descriptive. PLFS rows carry design-based intervals. ASUSE and ASI rows do not, because the establishment-side tables publish no cell variances.

## Current instruments (checked July 2026)

| Instrument | Source and page | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Additional Capital Subsidy for Scaling Up | MSME Policy Note 2025-26, printed p.37 (PDF p.40) | Micro to small or medium, and small to medium, through expansion or diversification. 5% additional capital subsidy, maximum ₹25 lakh. |
| Payroll subsidy (EPF reimbursement) | MSME Policy Note 2025-26, printed p.38 (PDF p.40), section 2.3.2 | Employer's EPF contribution reimbursed for the first three years if the unit employs more than 20 persons. Ceiling ₹24,000 per employee per annum. |
| Capital subsidy (general): reported output | MSME Policy Note 2025-26, printed p.38 (PDF p.40) | "During 2024-25, a subsidy of Rs.359.99 Crore has been disbursed to 2,481 enterprises." The sentence closes section 2.3.1 "Capital Subsidy". It is not a payroll-subsidy or scale-up figure. |

The capital-subsidy figure is an output measure: money disbursed and a count of recipients. It does not show recipient growth, additional employment, counterfactual behaviour, the job quality of supported jobs, or displacement of non-recipients.

The payroll-subsidy eligibility wording is "more than 20 persons" (strictly greater than 20).

## Historical objectives

The Tamil Nadu Industrial Policy 2021 (printed p.4, PDF p.9) sets these targets:

- "Achieve annual growth rate of 15% in the manufacturing sector during the policy term."
- "Attract investments worth INR 10 lakh cr. (US$ 135 billion) between 2020 and 2025."
- "Create employment opportunities for 20 lakh (2 million) people by 2025."
- "Increase the contribution of the Manufacturing Sector to 30% of GSVA by 2030"

The validity statement is on printed p.3 (PDF p.8): "This Policy supersedes the Tamil Nadu Industrial Policy 2014 and will be valid till 31.03.2025."

These are historical stated objectives. They are not current targets.

The 2025-26 Budget Speech shows that investment, MSME lending, industrial parks and job creation remain active concerns. It announces, for example, the Tamil Nadu Semiconductor Mission-2030 with a five-year allocation of ₹500 crore (p.49). It does not renew the expired 2021 Industrial Policy targets.

## Successor check (July 2026)

Web sources checked in July 2026 show no enacted successor policy, no extension order and no notified new general Industrial Policy. The 2021 policy text says it is valid till 31.03.2025 (printed p.3). No amendment was found on official mirrors. The investingintamilnadu.com portal still lists the 2021 policy unchanged. A portal listing does not show that a policy remains in force.

The newer documents are narrow sectoral policies. None succeeds the general policy: Circular Economy Investment Policy 2026, Shipbuilding Policy 2026, Textile Policy 2025-26, Space Industrial Policy 2025 (Cabinet-approved 18 April 2025) and Toy Manufacturing Policy 2025.

The only forward-looking evidence is a statement of intent. The Governor's address to the Assembly on 18 June 2026 said the government "would unveil" a "new industrial policy outlining special incentives for domestic and global companies" and set up an "Investor Promotion Commission". It cited a target of making Tamil Nadu "a 1.5 trillion dollar economy by 2036" (UNI, 2026-06-18: https://www.uniindia.com/tn-to-unveil-new-industrial-policy-to-set-up-investor-promotion-commission-to-attract-foreign-investments/south/news/3880521.html, with the same-day report on webindia123.com). This is a future-tense statement of intent. No policy has been notified.

Treatment: the 2021 targets are historical stated objectives (level 1, expired). As of July 2026, no current general manufacturing target framework could be verified. Current instruments (the MSME Policy Note 2025-26, budget allocations and sectoral policies) do exist. The announced policy is an intent and is not an instrument.

### Findings with no matching current instrument

Findings 3 and 5, and the contract and paid-leave components of finding 4, map to no verified current stated objective. This is a result in its own right. The state's verified current instruments target investment, category graduation, EPF-covered hiring and sectoral development. They do not target wage levels, labour share, written contracts or paid leave.

### Two focused policy tests

1. **Scale-transition gap test (finding 1 against the scale-up subsidy).** The state subsidizes category graduation, and the descriptive evidence shows no unusually thin middle. Statewide size scarcity is therefore not an adequate justification by itself. A justification may still rest on productivity, formalisation, or regional or sector-specific grounds, which the current data cannot test.

2. **20-worker threshold diagnostic (finding 6 against the payroll subsidy).** This describes structure around the threshold and social-security coverage by broad enterprise size. It is a threshold diagnostic. It is not a regression-discontinuity evaluation. Frames differ near the threshold, PLFS sizes are broad categories, recipients cannot be identified, and reporting may respond to legal thresholds. The EPF reimbursement does not establish that written contracts or paid leave are explicit government priorities. The measured outcomes that bear on the instrument are employment structure around the 20-person level and movement into EPF-covered (specified social-security) employment.

## Budget Speech 2025-26 evidence

The Budget Speech 2025-26 shows investment, parks, MSME lending and job creation as current concerns. Every statement is a level 1 or 2 statement with announced outputs. None reports outcome or impact evidence.

- §107, p.49: "As the next step, the Government will launch and implement the 'Tamil Nadu Semiconductor Mission-2030' over the next five years with an estimated allocation of Rs.500 crore." Also a Semiconductor Fabless Lab in Chennai, ₹100 crore.
- §109, p.50: Tidel Park Hosur and mini-Tidel Virudhunagar, ₹400 crore, "employment for 6,600 people."
- §111, pp.51-52: two footwear parks (Melur and Cuddalore), ₹250 crore, with 10,000 + 10,000 jobs; an engineering and fabrication park in Trichy, 250 acres, 5,000 jobs.
- §113, p.53: "Tamil Nadu Maritime Transport Manufacturing Policy 2025", a new sectoral policy for shipbuilding, projected to create 30,000 jobs in Cuddalore and Thoothukudi. It is sectoral and is not a successor to the 2021 general Industrial Policy.
- §115, p.54: Industries, Investment Promotion and Commerce Department allocation ₹3,915 crore.
- §116, p.54: MSME lending for FY 2025-26: banks to lend to 10 lakh MSMEs, outlay ₹2.5 lakh crore.
- §117, p.55: nine new SIDCO industrial estates, ₹366 crore, "17,500 employment opportunities."

Announced job numbers are projections attached to instruments (level 2). They are not measured outcomes (level 4). The speech contains no extension of the 2021 Industrial Policy.

## Instrument-to-evidence mapping

| Policy instrument | What government currently reports | What this project can measure | What remains unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capital subsidy for scaling up | Subsidy rules and aggregate disbursement | Establishment and employment shares across size categories | Whether recipients actually graduated because of the subsidy |
| Payroll subsidy above 20 employees | Eligibility and aggregate recipients and expenditure | Employment distribution around 20 workers; EPF and social-security coverage | Additional hiring caused by the subsidy |
| MSME lending targets | Aggregate lending amounts and borrower counts | ASUSE loan access and reported finance problems | Whether credit causally increases growth |
| Industrial parks and cluster facilities | Parks created, expenditure and announced jobs | Sector and geographic employment structure where data permit | Whether parks caused additional investment or employment |
| Investment incentives | Sanctions, investment commitments and disbursement | ASI capital, employment and GVA trends | Counterfactual outcomes without incentives |

## Interpretation boundaries

- An instrument's existence does not imply take-up, demand or impact.
- Adjusted peer gaps (METHODOLOGY.md) are descriptive composition controls. Pairwise weights rule out league-table readings across comparators.
- The supported conclusion is that Tamil Nadu's current MSME policy architecture explicitly supports micro-to-small and small-to-medium scaling, and subsidizes EPF contributions for eligible establishments employing more than 20 people. Investment and employment are visible priorities in current budget announcements.
- The survey evidence does not support the claim that officials consider the missing middle their primary manufacturing problem, or that they prioritize peer-leading wages, contracts, paid leave and job quality.
- Interviews are needed before presenting any of this as answering an official decision need. Until then, this note links findings to documents only.
- Suppressed cells remain suppressed. This note and the ASUSE problems appendix show no suppressed estimate.
